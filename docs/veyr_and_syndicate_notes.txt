I'd make Veyr capture an entirely different experience from claiming a human ship. With human vessels, disabling the ship is usually the hard part. With Veyr, disabling it is only the beginning.

Veyr vessel recovery
1. Disable the vessel

The player must destroy or disable critical systems without completely destroying the central biological core. This is especially difficult because Veyr ships don't have conventional engines, reactors, or control systems.

2. Identify the self-destruct mechanism

Once disabled, the player has a limited window to scan the vessel and identify its self-destruct mechanism. The mechanism could vary between ship classes, so the player can't simply memorize one procedure.

3. Attempt containment

The player must use specialized equipment to interrupt the sequence. Possible equipment upgrades could include:

Neural Disruptor: Interferes with the Veyr's biological command network.

Bioelectric Dampener: Suppresses the energy buildup feeding the self-destruct.

Core Stabilizer: Keeps the central biological core from entering a catastrophic failure state.

Containment Interface: Allows the player to interact with unfamiliar Veyr systems.

These would be expensive, specialized pieces of equipment, probably unavailable until the mid-game.

4. Escape or survive the detonation

If the attempt fails, the ship detonates. The blast should be substantially more powerful than a human vessel's reactor explosion.

Critical threat A nearby player ship could suffer serious hull and subsystem damage, while nearby fighters might be destroyed outright.

The player should receive some warning through scanner readings, visual effects, and audio cues, but not so much warning that escaping is guaranteed.

The reward
Successfully capturing a Veyr vessel should feel like winning the lottery. There are some interesting consequences worth considering:

Veyr vessels cannot be serviced at ordinary shipyards.

Human technicians initially have very little understanding of their systems.

Recovered vessels require specialized research and maintenance facilities.

Their weapons and propulsion systems cannot simply be swapped for human equipment.

Captured vessels could eventually become extraordinarily powerful additions to the player's fleet.

I'd also make the larger vessels progressively harder to recover. A Skitter might be an achievable early experiment, while a Reaver is a serious undertaking. A Broodguard or Leviathan should be an enormous late-game project, potentially requiring a dedicated salvage operation and multiple support ships.

One particularly interesting possibility
A captured Veyr vessel shouldn't necessarily be completely safe.

Even after successful recovery, its biological systems might remain partially active. The player could experience unpredictable behavior: dormant appendages moving, unexplained energy spikes, defensive systems activating, or the vessel attempting to regenerate damaged tissue.

That creates a wonderful distinction between owning a Veyr ship and actually controlling one.

That's exactly the sort of emergent gameplay that makes a sandbox space game memorable.

The Veyr recovery and control system
I'd establish three distinct challenges: capture, stabilization, and integration.

1. Capture: keep the organism alive
The player must disable the Veyr ship without destroying its central biological core. Even then, the ship may initiate its self-destruct sequence.

A successful capture requires specialized equipment to interrupt the biological command network and stabilize its energy systems. Ordinary ship repair tools won't work.

2. Stabilization: find someone who understands it
This is where the black market becomes essential.

I'd introduce a network of underground specialists, each with different capabilities:

Not every shady station should offer everything. The really capable people should be hidden deep in pirate territory.

3. Integration: make it obey
Even after stabilization, the vessel isn't necessarily under the player's control. Its biological systems need to be conditioned, suppressed, or interfaced with.

I'd make this a progression with several upgrades:

Neural Suppression: Prevents the vessel from rejecting external commands.

Bio-Energetic Regulator: Makes its power output predictable.

Synaptic Interface: Allows the player's fleet command system to communicate with it.

Regenerative Control Matrix: Allows controlled biological regeneration without unwanted mutations or system failures.

Alien Systems Monitor: Provides meaningful information about subsystem health, something normal human diagnostic equipment cannot do.

Early on, the player might only be able to keep a captured Veyr ship dormant in a specialized facility. Later, they can bring it into service, and eventually operate it as a fully integrated fleet vessel.

One mechanic I'd particularly like
Veyr ships should have maintenance requirements that human vessels don't.

A human ship can sit in a hangar for months without anyone worrying about whether its hull is alive. A Veyr ship might require biological nutrient compounds, controlled energy exposure, and periodic neural stabilization.

Neglecting those requirements could cause:

Regenerative tissue to grow over damaged components.

Weapons to become unresponsive.

The ship to enter a defensive state.

Its biological core to become unstable.

In extreme cases, another self-destruct attempt.

That makes owning one a serious commitment rather than simply acquiring an overpowered ship.

And there's a great gameplay opportunity here: the player could eventually become one of the very few independent operators in the galaxy capable of maintaining Veyr vessels. That reputation alone could open up some very interesting missions and relationships.

I'd develop the Outer Rim Syndicates next, including their internal factions, reputation levels, black-market services, and the specific technology progression required to recover and operate Veyr vessels.
